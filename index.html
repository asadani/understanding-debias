FIRST, NAME THE FAILURE

“Bias” can mean
three different things.

Pick the symptom you can measure. A direction shared by vectors, a candidate that wins too often, and a mistaken training negative are not the same failure.

A

Directions bunch together

Many embeddings occupy a narrow cone or share dominant components. This is often called anisotropy. Mean subtraction or removing top components may help, if the shared directions are nuisance variation.

↗↗↗→one crowded direction
Representation geometry · inspect first
B

One candidate wins everywhere

A point retrieved by many unrelated queries is a hub. This is a neighbourhood / scoring problem. CSLS and local scaling compare a match against its local similarity background.

•↗↗↘↙→many queries, one hub
Ranking geometry · count top-k hits
C

Training calls a true match “negative”

Contrastive training can push apart two texts that belong to the same intent. A false negative is a sampling/objective problem. Debiased contrastive methods adjust learning; they do not bolt onto frozen scores.

card not working← pushed apart →card keeps declining
Training objective · audit pair labels

Words such as “finance” or “manager” pointing in a similar direction do not by themselves prove bias. First decide what the category means: function, industry, seniority, issue type, or customer intent. Then inspect actual errors and neighbourhoods.

THE IMPORTANT GEOMETRY CHECK

For ordinary Euclidean distance, centering both a point and every centroid by the same mean changes nothing:

‖(x − μ) − (ck − μ)‖₂ = ‖x − ck‖₂

For cosine, re-normalising after centering can change angles. Removing directions can change either geometry. The transform must match the distance rule used at inference.

A SMALL, HONEST LAB

What if “generic”
is the whole problem?

For one invented message, raw cosine slightly prefers “payment help”. But that cluster also scores highly for many unrelated messages. A simple background correction asks how much of this score might be generic.

A CANDIDATE BASELINE CORRECTION
adjusted(q,k) = cosine(q,ck) − λ · bk
q new messageck cluster prototypebk average background similarity

This is a hypothesis, not a universal algorithm. The correction strength λ must be selected on held-out validation data. A wider margin does not mean the new ranking is correct.

DISCOVER → MEASURE → FREEZE

Freezing is the
release step.

A frozen model makes category assignment stable. It also preserves mistakes. Check the pipeline before calling the boundary “better”.

01 · DEFINE

Choose one axis

Intent, issue type, job function, industry or seniority. Write examples that should stay together and examples that should separate.

02 · DISCOVER

Cluster the corpus

Embed the training corpus. Explore clusters, duplicates, frequent neighbours and the documents nearest each centre. Human-review the taxonomy.

03 · COMPARE

Test corrections

Keep train, validation and test separate. Compare a modern embedding baseline against calibrated prototypes, local scaling, and any justified embedding transform.

04 · RELEASE

Freeze the full recipe

Version the encoder, instructions, preprocessing, centres, corrections, thresholds, taxonomy and reference snapshot together.

Do not silently force every message into a cluster. Choose a rejection rule from validation examples and monitor drift. A new issue that does not fit the frozen taxonomy needs a path to review and a versioned taxonomy update.

A RESEARCH TRAIL, NOT A VICTORY LAP

Old ideas.
Current questions.

A short chronology shows how the topic branches. Each paper answers a narrower question than “does debiasing work?”

  1. 2010

    Hubs in high-dimensional spaces

    Formalises the tendency of some points to become neighbours of many others. The general geometry predates modern transformer embeddings.

    Radovanović, Nanopoulos & Ivanović · JMLR ↗
    FOUNDATION
  2. 2017

    All-but-the-Top

    Subtracts a global mean and removes dominant principal components from word vectors. Evidence on word/sentence representation tasks; not automatic proof for modern job-title encoders.

    Mu, Bhat & Viswanath · ICLR 2018 ↗
    REPRESENTATION
  3. 2017–18

    CSLS for cross-lingual retrieval

    Corrects similarity using local neighbourhood density. It changes retrieval ranking; it does not alter an encoder’s training or discover clusters.

    Conneau et al. · ICLR 2018 ↗
    RANKING
  4. 2020

    Debiased contrastive learning

    Corrects for same-class examples sampled as negatives during self-supervised training. This “debias” has a distinct meaning from subtracting a cluster baseline at inference.

    Chuang et al. · NeurIPS 2020 ↗
    TRAINING
  5. 2022

    DCLR for sentence representations

    Uses instance weighting and noise-based negatives; evaluates seven semantic-textual-similarity tasks. A useful bridge to NLP, though still not a job-taxonomy result.

    Zhou et al. · ACL 2022 ↗
    NLP
  6. 2023–24

    Hubness reduction in Sentence-BERT spaces

    Study reports reduced hubness and neighbourhood-classifier error when hubness is high. The paper explicitly measures its evaluated pretrained models; it does not guarantee gains on every dataset.

    Nielsen & Hansen · NLDL 2024 ↗
    TEXT EMBEDDINGS
  7. 2024–25

    False negatives stay a live training issue

    Modern retrieval recipes still mine hard negatives and filter examples that may actually be relevant. Newer is not “solved”: label quality and the target domain matter.

    NV-Retriever · 2024 · current Sentence Transformers guide ↗
    PRACTICE
  8. 2026

    Hubness vs anisotropy in multilingual retrieval

    A recent arXiv preprint reports hubness, rather than anisotropy, as the main driver of a particular multilingual retrieval asymmetry. Its scope is five encoders and four languages; independent replication and your task’s evaluation still matter.

    Sakhawat et al. · arXiv preprint ↗
    CURRENT PREPRINT
Why does a 2017 method still appear in a modern guide?

Because the mechanism is still testable, not because old numbers transfer. Use a contemporary encoder as the starting point, diagnose your task’s failure mode, then compare simple post-processing and local ranking methods on held-out examples. Retire a correction if it fails your benchmark.

FIRST HELD-OUT MEASUREMENT

What did the held-out test say?

On five closely related BANKING77 intents, a validation-tuned mean-background offset raised test accuracy by 0.5 percentage points and macro-F1 by 0.52 points. CSLS lowered both by about 0.5 points. That is a small, task-specific result, not proof of a general “debias” improvement.
